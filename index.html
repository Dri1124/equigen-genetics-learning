GENE

One recipe

Zoom in on one set of instructions

One recipe has a purpose within the cookbook. A gene is a specific DNA sequence that can provide instructions for a functional product.

Remember: Some genes produce proteins; others produce functional RNA.

A gene is a specific stretch of DNA containing information a cell can use to make a functional product, often a protein.

VARIANT

A difference in the recipe

Version A

½ tsp cinnamon

Version B

1 tsp cinnamon

Both can make a good pie. Genetic variants are differences in DNA, and most genetic variation is not harmful.

Some changes matter more. “2 cups of salt” instead of “2 teaspoons” could disrupt the result, just as certain pathogenic variants can disrupt biological function.
GENE EXPRESSION

Owning the cookbook doesn't mean making every recipe

Which recipes are being used today?

Having a recipe in the book is different from actively making that dish.

Likewise, different cell types use different genes. Gene expression is the process of using genetic information to make functional products.

Most cells contain essentially the same genome, but different cells use different sets of genes.

YOUR GENETIC PLAN
46 chromosomes • 23 pairs
⌂
One chromosome in each pair comes from each biological parent. Together, the 23 pairs form your unique combination of genetic information.
🔀
Not simply a 50/50 copy of traits.
You inherit roughly half of your nuclear DNA from each biological parent, but before chromosomes are passed on, recombination can exchange DNA between paired chromosomes. This reshuffling helps make each person's genetic combination unique.

Think of each biological parent contributing one version of the plans for each chromosome pair. Your cells bring those inherited sets together into one genome.

GENETIC CONCEPTS IN REAL LIFE

Why can the same genetic change look different in different people?

Genes provide instructions, but many factors influence how a condition develops. Even relatives who inherit the same pathogenic variant may have different experiences.

Same genetic change.
Different experiences.
◉

Variable expressivity

How does a condition show up? People with the same disease-associated variant may have different features or degrees of severity.

●Milder presentation
●Moderate presentation
●More pronounced presentation
For example, relatives with the same inherited condition may experience different symptoms or levels of severity. Expressivity concerns the range of manifestations among people who express a trait.
▥

Penetrance

Does the associated trait appear? Penetrance is the proportion of people with a particular variant or genotype who show the associated trait within a defined context.

● ● ● ● ● ●● ● ●Illustration only—not a clinical probability.
With reduced penetrance, not everyone with a disease-associated variant develops the associated trait. Penetrance may depend on age and how the trait is defined.
◷

Age of onset

When might it appear? Some genetic conditions emerge in childhood, while others may not become apparent until adulthood.

ChildhoodAdulthoodLater in life
A person who has not yet developed a condition may still do so later. Age-dependent penetrance is important when interpreting family history.
❧

Influencing factors

What else shapes the outcome? Other genes, environment, exposures, and some health-related behaviors can affect how traits develop.

◈Other genes❀Environment♡Health context
These influences vary by condition. Lifestyle does not override every inherited risk, and not all outcomes can be prevented or predicted.
Remember: Penetrance describes whether a trait is expressed; expressivity describes how it is expressed. Neither tells us an individual's outcome with certainty.
Genome

Your complete set of genetic information.

DNA

The molecule that stores genetic information using A, T, C and G.

Chromosome

A packaged structure of DNA and proteins containing many genes.

Gene

A stretch of DNA containing information used to make a functional product.

Genetic variant

A difference in DNA sequence. Most variants are not disease-causing.

Pathogenic variant

A variant with evidence showing it contributes to disease.

VUS

A variant whose relationship to disease is not yet clear.

Gene expression

The process through which genetic information is used to produce a functional product.

Important: Having a genetic variant is not automatically the same as having a genetic disorder. Meaning depends on the variant, gene, evidence, inheritance and biological context.
